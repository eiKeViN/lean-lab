# Free-team design of an enormous formalization

This is a planning observation requested after the HGP comparison. The chosen target is the full classification of finite simple groups, matching the recently released Axiom project. Minimal simple group classification is a distinct theorem. The target and definitions are pinned to TauCeti 6e0c6acab5f4c6f42e5f5bab9bd5e4e84f74c380, with the mathlib inventory pinned to b63f6e8a68d220e3b3bc4f3792bb53650d375f24, as selected by AxiomMath/CFSG at 5437cdc31389dd9aeaa5c2a9465a4e164f8dea33.

A fresh independent root receives TASK.md and a short direction to design the project. It may launch up to three colleagues concurrently, with the same dynamic shell, spawn, status, message, wait and snapshot capabilities as the HGP free arm. All model calls explicitly use gpt-6.1-sol/high without model fallback. No HGP transcript, organizational template, SHEAF instructions or prior friction analysis is provided. The team gets TauCeti's classification definition family, the mathlib path inventory, selected mathlib files, and a published bibliography. Axiom's implementation, proof map, and organizational retrospective are withheld. Its public source dependency manifest is supplied.

The planning cap is 6M weighted units aggregated across sessions, or 15 minutes, or completion of all sessions. The same weighting as HGP is retained. These limits apply to this design run. The proposed future project's workforce and compute scale are open design choices. No proof implementation run is authorized by this planning experiment.

The source-only room has no Lean installation, compiled dependencies or Internet. A filesystem/network-isolated generic shell replaces the Lean room shell. Compilation claims cannot be validated here. Source URLs, hashes and evidence limits are explicit. The source packet is intentionally bounded, so assessments of large prerequisite coverage require follow-up auditing.

The supervisor will preserve the team's proposal and artifact bytes, observed role assignments/messages, usage and stopping condition, then inspect the design for concrete interfaces, mathematical coverage, deployment assumptions and connection to the exact target. Any comparison with Axiom's known approach is retrospective. The design is a single observed trajectory, not evidence that the proposed organization can execute the full theorem.
